Spacing

mads.space

The spacing scale used for padding, margins and gaps between elements. It sits on an 8px grid and doubles at each step from 8px upward.

Namingmads.space.{0–8}

Major grid · 8px

Always pick a step from the scale first. Steps 2–8 are 8px multiples.

Minor grid · 4px

When no step fits, use a multiple of 4px (12, 24, 48…). Use mads.space.1 for tight gaps such as icon to label.

Units

1 web px = 1 iOS pt = 1 Android dp. Web uses rem at a 16px root, so spacing follows the user's text size.

Web

Loaded from Google Fonts with display=swap, so text shows straight away in the fallback font and switches once the font arrives.

iOS

Bundle the font files in the app. If one is missing, the code uses the matching Apple font: SF Pro Rounded, SF Pro or SF Mono.

Android

Downloaded through Google Play services. Until they arrive, or if they can't, Android uses its own sans-serif and monospace fonts.

Text

mads.text

Four sizes for text, labels, captions, footnotes and all other copy that isn't a heading. Each style sets a font size and a line height, and uses mads.font.body unless you pair it with another font.

Namingmads.text.{small | default | large | xlarge}
Click any sample to type your own text
TokenSize / lineWebiOSAndroid

Labels

For labels, use the Labels styles instead. They use mads.font.mono in capitals.

Scaling

Sizes follow the user's text size setting: rem on web, Dynamic Type on iOS and sp on Android. Each iOS style scales with the Apple style of the same size.

Units

Sizes are given in px. That is pt on iOS and sp on Android, where text uses sp rather than dp.

Headings

mads.heading

Seven heading sizes, from a hero title down to the smallest section heading. Every heading uses mads.font.display (Unbounded) in bold. The hero size grows with the screen width.

Namingmads.heading.{xsmall | small | default | large | xlarge | xxlarge | hero}
Click any sample to type your own text
TokenSize / lineHTMLWebiOSAndroid

Weight

All sizes use Bold (700), set once in --mads-heading-weight. Hero is ExtraBold (800) with −0.03em tracking.

HTML levels

The suggested level keeps the page outline in order: xxlarge for h1 down to xsmall for h6. hero is also an h1, used once at the top of a landing page instead of xxlarge. Choose the level by structure and the size by look.

Units

Sizes are given in px: rem on web, pt on iOS and sp on Android, so headings follow the user's text size setting.

Colours

mads.color

Nine colours: four neutrals for text and structure, and five bright accents. Each swatch shows how readable white and Dark text are on top of it. Click a swatch to copy its hex value.

Namingmads.color.{name}

Neutrals

Accents

TokenHexRGBWebiOSAndroid

Contrast

AA means normal text is readable (4.5:1 or more). Large means only text from 24px, or 19px bold, is readable (3:1 or more). Below 3:1, use the colour for fills and shapes, not text.

Same everywhere

Hex values are sRGB and identical on web, iOS and Android. They don't change between light and dark mode yet.

Names

Tokens use lowercase words joined by hyphens, so AmberGold is mads.color.amber-gold. Swift and Kotlin use amberGold.

Themes

mads.theme

Colour roles that switch between light and dark mode. Components use these roles instead of fixed colours, so they follow the theme. Use the switch at the top of the page to see every component in the other mode.

Namingmads.theme.{role}

Contrast is measured against each theme's background. AA means normal text is readable; Large means only text from 24px, or 19px bold.

TokenLightDarkUse for

Following the device

Without a choice, the theme follows the device setting. The switch overrides it and the choice is remembered.

Accent text

Bright accents are too light to read on a light background, so each has a darker text version for light mode. Use these for coloured text; use mads.color for fills.

Shared colours

Light text and muted text are mads.color.dark and mads.color.dark-1. The other values are new and only exist as theme roles.

Direction

Left to right for controls, text and bars. Top to bottom for frames and the logo legs. 160° for surface fades. Ambient glows are radial.

How many

One gradient action and one highlighted phrase per screen. The ambient glow is always there, but stays at 16% in light mode and 22% in dark.

Contrast

Text on a gradient is only allowed on the primary button. White on the orange-pink gradient is below 4.5:1, so keep button labels short.

Motion

mads.motion

Durations and easing curves shared by every animated element, so the whole interface moves the same way. The values match the existing buttons and icons.

Namingmads.motion.duration.{fast | base | slow} · mads.motion.easing.{standard | spring | entrance}
TokenValueUse for

Pick the curve

Standard for state changes such as hover and colour. Spring for things that move into place, like the switch knob. Entrance for elements arriving on screen.

Scroll reveal

Sections rise 48px into place as they scroll in, with mads-reveal. Content is always visible where the browser does not support it.

Reduced motion

When the device asks for less motion, every animation stops and states change instantly.

Contrast

White on BlazeOrange is 3.26:1 and on NeonPink 3.83:1. Both are below the 4.5:1 needed for 15px text, so the label may be hard to read for some people.

Icons

An optional icon sits after the text, 1em in size and in the text colour. On hover it nudges 1px in the direction it points.

Motion

Hover takes 0.6s, press 0.08s. With reduced motion turned on, states change instantly.

Secondary, ghost, icon and round buttons

Paired with the primary button and built from the theme roles, so they work in light and dark mode.

TokenValueNotes

Secondary

For the second action next to a primary button, such as Get in touch beside the main call to action. Same size, text and shape as the primary.

Ghost

For navigation links and low-emphasis actions. Mono text, no fill until hover. Mark the current page with aria-current.

Icon

A 40 circle holding a 20px MADS icon, for previous/next and other icon-only actions. Always give it an accessible name.

Round

The secondary button as a 44 circle with a 20px MADS icon and no text, for actions such as Settings next to other secondary buttons. Same fill, line, hover, press and focus as the secondary. Always give it an accessible name.

Popovers

mads.popover

A round button that opens a floating card: the Ideas and Settings menus in the header of this page and of miguel-adan.com. The card grows out of its button, its rows slide in one after the other, and only one card is open at a time. The samples below are the same components the header uses.

Namingmads.popover · mads.popover.{row | link} · mads.language.switch
TokenValueNotes

Behaviour

Pressing the button opens its card and closes any other. A second press, a tap or click outside the card, Esc or moving the focus out of it closes it again; Esc puts the focus back on the button.

Motion

The card grows out of the button as a circle while it settles 8 down into place (slow, entrance), then its rows slide in 10 up, 80ms apart. Settings turns 120° with the spring; Ideas turns AmberGold and plays its glow.

Accessibility

The button carries aria-expanded and aria-controls; the card is a labelled group. With reduced motion the card simply appears. The language switch is a link with hreflang, or greyed out with aria-disabled when a page has one language.
